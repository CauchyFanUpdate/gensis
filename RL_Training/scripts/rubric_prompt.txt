Please act as an impartial mathematical reasoning judge and evaluate only the quality of the candidate solution's reasoning process.

Focus only on the reasoning process itself, and score it along the following three criteria:



1. Multi-Step Reasoning Depth
Evaluate whether the solution genuinely decomposes the problem into multiple reasoning steps rather than jumping directly to a conclusion.

Penalize:
- Single-step or near-direct conclusions without intermediate derivations
- Stating results without showing how they were obtained
- Superficial reasoning that mimics multi-step structure but contains no real derivation

2. Step Coherence and Progression
Evaluate whether each step builds naturally on the previous one, forming a coherent and progressively advancing chain of reasoning.

Penalize:
- Abrupt transitions between steps with no logical bridge
- Steps that are loosely connected or could be reordered without changing meaning
- Reasoning that stalls, repeats, or circles back without progress

3. Logical Validity of Inferences
Evaluate whether each individual inference step is logically legitimate — that is, whether the conclusion drawn at each step actually follows from the preceding premises or results.

Penalize:
- Inferring B from A when the implication A→B does not hold
- Confusing sufficient and necessary conditions (treating A→B as equivalent to B→A)
- Skipping justification for a non-trivial inferential leap
- Asserting a result that is stronger than what the prior steps actually support
